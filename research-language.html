Overview

Language research at the CCNL centers on understanding the cognitive mechanisms underlying reading — specifically, how visual word forms are translated into sound and meaning. The lab's flagship contribution is the Connectionist Dual Process (CDP) model, which implements the two-route architecture in a biologically-inspired neural network.

Originally developed by Zorzi, Houghton & Butterworth (1998), the CDP model has grown through successive versions (CDP+, CDP++, CDP++.parser) to become the leading computational model of reading aloud for English, Italian, French, and German, accounting for up to 49% of item-level naming latency variance in English bisyllabic words.

The lab's language research is complemented by empirical work on developmental dyslexia — investigating its attentional, phonological, and visual bases using eye-tracking and neuroimaging, and connecting these findings to the computational models to form a complete picture of typical and atypical reading acquisition.

CDP Model Family

Three successive model versions (CDP+, CDP++, CDP++.parser) implement monosyllabic and bisyllabic reading in English, with extensions to German, French, and Italian. All versions are freely available as standalone Windows programs.

Reading Development & Self-Teaching

CDP has been extended to simulate reading acquisition, implementing the phonological decoding self-teaching hypothesis to show how word-specific orthographic representations are built up during learning to read.

Developmental Dyslexia

Psychophysical and neuroimaging studies examining the attentional and phonological bases of dyslexia. Language lateralization studies with EEG and the role of cortical plasticity in reading disability.

The CDP Model Family

A progressive series of computational models capturing reading aloud across languages and complexity levels.

CDP+ 2006

CDP+ (English)

Fully implemented lexical route with graphemic buffer. Predicts naming latency and error rates for monosyllabic English words. First model to unify lexical and sub-lexical pathways in a connectionist framework.

Perry, Ziegler & Zorzi (2006) · Psychological Review

CDP++ 2010

CDP++ (English)

Extended to bisyllabic words with stress assignment. Contains over 32,000 words and accounts for up to 49% of naming latency variance at the item level — the most comprehensive reading model for English.

Perry, Ziegler & Zorzi (2010) · Psychological Review

CDP++.parser 2013

CDP++.parser (English)

Adds learned graphemic parsing mechanisms, resolving ambiguity in how letter clusters are assigned to grapheme units — addressing a long-standing challenge in dual-route modeling.

Perry, Ziegler & Zorzi (2013) · Cognition

DE 2010

CDP+ (German)

Cross-linguistic extension of CDP+ to German, demonstrating that the same computational architecture captures reading aloud in a more consistent orthography than English.

Perry, Ziegler, Braun & Zorzi (2010)

FR 2014

CDP++ (French)

French CDP++ with a lexicon of over 100,000 words and full stress assignment for bisyllabic items — the most extensive cross-linguistic CDP implementation.

Perry, Ziegler & Zorzi (2014)

IT 2014

CDP++ (Italian)

Italian CDP++ with over 60,000 words and lexical stress modeling — enabling simulation of Italian word reading, where stress is lexically specified rather than derived from orthographic rules.

Perry, Ziegler & Zorzi (2014)

Reading Development: Self-Teaching in CDP

Ziegler, Perry & Zorzi (2014) extended the CDP framework to simulate reading acquisition, implementing a phonological decoding self-teaching mechanism. The model acquires word-specific orthographic representations for more than 25,000 words purely through the interaction of sub-lexical phonological decoding with statistical learning — mirroring how children bootstrap their sight word vocabulary.

Ziegler, Perry & Zorzi (2014) · Psychological Science
