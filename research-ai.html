Overview

AI research at the CCNL has a dual mandate: building AI systems that are genuinely illuminating about cognition, and using insights from cognitive science to build better AI. This has driven the lab's focus on biologically-inspired generative models — learning systems that, like the brain, discover structure in data through unsupervised experience.

The lab pioneered the use of Deep Belief Networks (DBNs) for cognitive modeling through the ERC-funded GENMOD project, demonstrating emergent cognitive abilities (such as number sense and letter recognition) from purely unsupervised learning — without pre-programmed representations.

Current research expands to large-scale multimodal foundation models: probing how vision-language models represent quantities, emotions, and abstract concepts — and whether their emergent capabilities reflect genuine cognitive competencies or sophisticated statistical pattern matching.

Neural Network Models of Cognition

Biologically-inspired architectures (DBNs, iDBNs) trained to simulate human perceptual and cognitive processes. Models are evaluated against behavioral and neuroimaging data to ensure cognitive plausibility, not just task performance.

Emergent Abilities in Large-Scale AI

Systematic investigations of what large language models and vision-language models "know" about quantities, space, and number — probing whether emergent abilities parallel genuine human cognitive competencies or are artefacts of training data statistics.

Machine Learning for Neuroinformatics

Applying deep learning as a tool for neuroimaging data analysis — including EEG seizure prediction, stroke lesion segmentation, and fMRI connectivity classification — bridging AI methodology with clinical neuroscience applications.

Research Highlights

Deep Belief Networks (DBNs)

Probabilistic generative models trained with contrastive divergence, building hierarchies of distributed representations through layerwise unsupervised learning. The CCNL developed optimized GPU/cluster implementations (Octave, MATLAB, Python) and the novel iDBN algorithm enabling joint layer updates.

Vision-Language Models & Numerosity

Current research by Sina Shafiezadeh examining multimodal AI and vision-language models with emphasis on modality gap — understanding differences between image and text embeddings regarding numerosity perception and numerical understanding across modalities.

Shafiezadeh & Zorzi · Ongoing

Numerosity Emergence from Unsupervised Learning

Landmark demonstration that training a DBN on natural images with objects spontaneously produces numerosity-sensitive units matching the signature Weber's Law properties of the human Approximate Number System — with no explicit number supervision.

Stoianov & Zorzi (2012) · Nature Neuroscience

Letter Recognition in Deep Networks

Deep unsupervised networks trained on natural images develop letter-like representations consistent with human letter confusion and similarity data. Confusion and similarity matrices released publicly for community benchmarking.

Testolin, Stoianov & Zorzi (2017)

iDBN: Iterative Deep Belief Networks

Novel developmental learning algorithm enabling joint weight updates across all DBN layers simultaneously, rather than greedy layerwise pre-training. Produces more coherent hierarchical representations and is available on the lab GitHub.

Zambra, Testolin & Zorzi (2023) · Cognitive Computation

AI for Neuroimaging & EEG

Deep learning applied to clinical neuroimaging: seizure prediction from EEG with cross-patient deep learning classifiers, stroke lesion analysis with machine learning, and fMRI connectivity classification — translating AI advances to neuroscience applications.

Shafiezadeh, De Filippo De Grazia & collaborators

ERC Advanced Grant

GENMOD Project (2008–2013)

"Generative models of human cognition" — a European Research Council Advanced Grant that established the CCNL's program of biologically-inspired deep learning for cognitive science. The project produced foundational work on DBNs for numerosity, reading, and word recognition, and developed the open-source computational tools that remain in active use by the community.
