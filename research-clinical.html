Overview

Clinical neuroscience at the CCNL bridges fundamental research with direct applications to human health. The lab studies neurological patients — primarily stroke survivors — to understand how brain damage disrupts cognition and how recovery can be supported through targeted rehabilitation.

A major focus is lesion-deficit mapping: identifying the neural substrates of specific cognitive functions by comparing patterns of impairment across patients with varying lesion profiles. This approach complements computational modeling by providing ground-truth data on the neural implementation of cognitive processes.

The lab's partnership with the IRCCS San Camillo Hospital in Venice provides privileged access to large patient cohorts and state-of-the-art clinical neuroimaging, enabling longitudinal studies of post-stroke recovery and rehabilitation efficacy.

Brain-Behavior Mapping

Systematic voxel-based lesion-symptom mapping (VLSM) and multivariate lesion analysis in stroke patients, identifying key cortical and white-matter structures whose damage predicts specific cognitive deficits.

Stroke Neuroimaging

Advanced structural and functional MRI protocols for characterizing post-stroke structural reorganization, diaschisis effects, and neural correlates of spontaneous recovery and intervention-driven plasticity.

Rehabilitation Methods

Development and assessment of advanced rehabilitation approaches for hemispatial neglect, including prism adaptation, non-invasive brain stimulation, and technology-assisted training with eye-tracking-based feedback systems.

Research Highlights

VLSM in Hemispatial Neglect

Large-scale voxel-based lesion-symptom mapping studies in stroke patients identifying the parietal-frontal network — particularly the right inferior frontal gyrus and superior longitudinal fasciculus — as critical for spatial attention. These findings inform targeted rehabilitation strategies for neglect.

Eye-tracking-based Rehabilitation

Novel computer-based rehabilitation tools using eye-tracking to deliver adaptive, real-time feedback to neglect patients. Machine learning classifiers distinguish neglect-related oculomotor patterns, enabling objective outcome monitoring across rehabilitation sessions.

Brain-Computer Interfaces

Collaborative work with Konstantinos Priftis on brain-computer interfaces guided by electrophysiological signals (EEG), with applications to motor rehabilitation and augmentative communication in neurological patients.

fNIRS & Cognitive Monitoring

Simone Cutini's fNIRS research providing portable, high-temporal-resolution monitoring of prefrontal and parietal activity during cognitive tasks — suitable for bedside assessment in clinical populations and ecological settings outside the scanner.

Dyscalculia Intervention

From basic research on developmental dyscalculia to clinical intervention: cognitive training programs and the La Gara dei Numeri game used in school and clinic settings, with controlled trials evaluating efficacy in children with diagnosed learning disabilities.

San Camillo Computational Neuroimaging

The Computational Neuroimaging Laboratory at IRCCS San Camillo Hospital provides the infrastructure for advanced fMRI analysis, diffusion tractography, and longitudinal rehabilitation studies in neurological patient populations, in direct collaboration with the CCNL.
