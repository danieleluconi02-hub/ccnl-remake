La Gara dei Numeri

The Number Race

Your brain can tell "more" from "less" without counting — this game trains that instinct.

Two quantities appear side by side. Choose the larger one, as fast as you can. The game tracks how quickly your brain resolves the comparison and adjusts difficulty in real time — harder when you're fast and accurate, easier when you hesitate. This targets the Approximate Number System, whose precision predicts arithmetic ability in children.

GARA records reaction time, accuracy, and difficulty level per trial. Authenticated users accumulate longitudinal score histories.

Neuro-Cognitive Adaptive Difficulty Ages 5–8 GPL Licensed
Technical Foundation — Estimation Precision & Weber's Law
$$w = \frac{\sigma(n)}{\mu(n)}$$ $$P(\text{correct}) = \Phi\!\left(\frac{\Delta n}{w \cdot \bar{n}}\right)$$ where: $\Delta n = |n_1 - n_2|$   (numerical distance) $\bar{n} = (n_1 + n_2)/2$   (mean numerosity) $w$ = Weber fraction ($\approx 0.15\text{–}0.30$) $\Phi$ = cumulative normal CDF Adaptive staircase: correct → difficulty $+= 0.025$ incorrect → difficulty $-= 0.050$ $\text{deadline} = \max(3000,\; 8000 - \text{difficulty} \times 4000)$ ms

About The Number Race

Original Game

"The Number Race" was developed by Anna J. Wilson, Stanislas Dehaene, Susannah K. Revkin, Dominique Cohen, Laurent Cohen, and Philippe Pinel (2006) at the INSERM-CEA Cognitive Neuroimaging Unit, Orsay, France.

Scientific Purpose

Designed for the remediation of developmental dyscalculia, the game is grounded in research on the neural foundations of numerical cognition, in particular the Approximate Number System (ANS) and its role in arithmetic learning.

Published under the GNU General Public License (GPL). Original game assets and validated cognitive mechanics are preserved in this web version.

DBN Explorer

Deep Belief Networks

A neural network learns to "see" numbers the same way a developing brain might — purely through observation, with no labels or instructions.

Stacked layers of simulated neurons adjust their connections to find recurring patterns in pixel inputs. The bottom layer detects edges and strokes; the top layer encodes abstract quantity. Draw any digit or dot pattern on the canvas and watch the network reconstruct its internal representation — the gap between input and reconstruction measures how well it has learned.

Training runs in your browser via Contrastive Divergence. Adjust η and k with the sliders below — watch the inspector for live reconstruction error.

Unsupervised Learning Generative Model Numerosity Nat. Neurosci. 2012

CDP Reading Model

Connectionist Dual Process

Reading a word activates two competing brain pathways at once — one recognises the whole word, the other sounds it out letter by letter.

Type any English word. The lexical route instantly retrieves the whole-word pronunciation from memory; the sub-lexical route assembles sounds letter by letter. The route diagram shows which pathway wins.

For irregular words (e.g. "have"), the routes conflict — the model predicts longer reaction times and higher error rates, exactly matching human data. Try a nonword like "splorn" to activate the sub-lexical route exclusively.

Dual-Route GPC Rules EN · IT · FR · DE Psych. Review 2007
Technical Foundation — Dual Route Integration
$$P(\text{word}) = \lambda \cdot P_{\text{lex}} + (1-\lambda) \cdot P_{\text{phon}}$$ Lexical route: $P_{\text{lex}} = f\!\left(\sum_{ij} w_{ij} \cdot a_i\right)$   (orthographic lexicon) Sub-lexical route: $P_{\text{phon}} = \text{GPC}(\text{grapheme} \to \text{phoneme})$ Integration: $\lambda = \sigma\!\left((\text{Zipf} - \theta) \cdot 1.2\right)$ $\text{output} = \arg\max\left[P(\text{word})\right]$ RT model (Perry et al. 2007, Table 2): $RT \approx 450 - 22(\text{freq}-3) + 18\max(0,\,L-3) + \delta_{\text{irreg}}$ ms
