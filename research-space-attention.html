Space and
Attention

Investigating the neural and computational mechanisms of visuospatial attention — from multitasking in healthy brains to neglect rehabilitation in stroke patients, and the surprising interactions between space, time, and number.

Meet the Researchers Eye-tracking Lab

Overview

Spatial attention is the cognitive mechanism that selectively prioritizes processing of information at specific locations in the visual field. The CCNL investigates how attention is deployed, maintained, and divided across space and time — and what happens when these mechanisms break down following brain injury.

A central theme is the study of hemispatial neglect, a severe attentional disorder that typically follows right-hemisphere stroke, in which patients fail to attend to information in the contralesional hemispace. The lab combines neuropsychological assessment with neuroimaging and brain stimulation to characterize neglect and develop novel rehabilitation approaches.

A second major focus is the interaction between spatial and numerical representations — captured in phenomena like the SNARC effect (Spatial-Numerical Association of Response Codes) — revealing deep connections between how the brain maps number and space.

Attention and Multitasking

Psychophysical and eye-tracking paradigms probing the limits of attentional resources when multiple objects or tasks compete for processing. Research by Sebastiano Cinetto combines eye-tracking with machine learning for stroke rehabilitation applications.

Space, Time and Number

Experimental work on the surprising links between spatial, temporal, and numerical processing — including the mental number line, SNARC effect, and how visuospatial biases modulate numerical estimation.

Neglect & Brain-Behavior Mapping

Systematic lesion-deficit mapping in stroke patients with hemispatial neglect. Studies use voxel-based morphometry, resting-state fMRI connectivity, and high-density EEG to identify the neural substrates of attentional failure and recovery.

Key Research Highlights

Hemispatial Neglect Characterization

Large-scale neuropsychological and neuroimaging studies in stroke patients with right-hemisphere damage, identifying key lesion sites and white-matter tract disruptions associated with hemispatial neglect severity and recovery.

Bonato, Priftis, Zorzi & collaborators

SNARC and Mental Number Line

Research on the spatial-numerical association of response codes, showing that small numbers are mentally represented on the left and large numbers on the right — and how this mapping is modulated by context, task, and individual differences.

Ranzini, Zorzi & collaborators

TMS & Non-invasive Brain Stimulation

Transcranial magnetic stimulation and transcranial electrical stimulation (TES) protocols used to causally investigate the role of right parietal and frontal areas in spatial attention, and to assess therapeutic potential in neglect.

Bonato & collaborators

Space-Time Interactions

Experimental investigation of how spatial and temporal processing interact at neural and behavioural levels — including STEARC effects, temporal order judgements, and the shared parietal resources for space and time representation.

Bonato, Ranzini & collaborators

Eye-tracking & Machine Learning

Combining high-resolution eye-tracking with machine learning classifiers to objectively characterize attentional biases in stroke patients, supporting both diagnosis and longitudinal monitoring of rehabilitation outcomes.

Cinetto, Bonato & collaborators

Default Mode & EEG Markers

Research by Zaira Romeo examining Default Mode Network dynamics and EEG/ERP markers of visuospatial attention — linking resting-state brain dynamics to individual differences in spatial cognition.

Romeo, Zorzi & collaborators

Methods & Facilities

Eye-tracking Lab

Two dedicated rooms with Tobii eye-trackers running E-Prime or OpenSesame for precise oculomotor and attentional measurement.

Neuroimaging & Stimulation

3T MRI (Philips Ingenia), 256-channel EEG, TMS, and TES protocols for both basic science and clinical populations.

San Camillo Partnership

Computational Neuroimaging Laboratory at IRCCS San Camillo Hospital, Venice — providing access to clinical stroke populations and advanced rehabilitation resources.
