Numerical

How the brain perceives, represents, and manipulates numbers — from innate number sense to mathematical learning and developmental dyscalculia.

Overview

The CCNL has a long-standing interest in understanding the (neuro)computational mechanisms underlying numerical cognition — the suite of abilities that allow humans to perceive, represent, and manipulate quantities and numbers. This spans from the most primitive sense of numerosity shared with other species, to uniquely human symbolic number knowledge and formal arithmetic.

A key achievement of the lab was demonstrating that numerosity perception emerges naturally from unsupervised deep learning (Stoianov & Zorzi, 2012, Nature Neuroscience), providing a computational account of the neural basis of the "number sense" — the innate ability to rapidly estimate quantities without counting.

The empirical wing of this research targets children with developmental dyscalculia, a specific learning disorder affecting mathematical abilities, using psychophysical paradigms and neuroimaging to characterize its cognitive and neural underpinnings and develop evidence-based interventions.

Numerosity Perception

Psychophysical studies and computational models of the Approximate Number System (ANS) — the core system for representing magnitudes in an analogue, noisy format. Covers Weber's Law in number estimation, numerosity adaptation, and the neural correlates of magnitude representation in parietal cortex.

Numerical Development

How children acquire symbolic number knowledge and arithmetic skills, including the role of the ANS in formal mathematics learning. Longitudinal and cross-sectional studies in children from pre-school to adolescence, examining how number sense predicts later mathematical achievement.

Developmental Dyscalculia

Characterizing the cognitive profile of dyscalculia and developing computational training tools. The La Gara dei Numeri (Italian Number Race) is a game-based intervention designed to strengthen number sense in children aged 4–8, addressing both typical and atypical numerical development.

Key Research Highlights

Emergent Number Sense in Deep Networks

Using Deep Belief Networks trained on visual scenes with objects, we demonstrated that numerosity perception — a hallmark of the Approximate Number System — emerges spontaneously from unsupervised learning, without any explicit number supervision.

Stoianov & Zorzi (2012) · Nature Neuroscience

Number Sense & Dyscalculia

Empirical and computational work identifying deficits in the approximate number system as a core marker of developmental dyscalculia, with implications for diagnosis and targeted intervention design in school-age children.

Sella, Zorzi & colleagues · Multiple venues

La Gara dei Numeri

An Italian adaptation of "The Number Race" (Wilson et al., 2006) — a free, adaptive video game for children aged 4–8 that strengthens number sense through engaging gameplay and evidence-based number comparison tasks, usable as a dyscalculia intervention.

Based on Wilson et al. (2006) · Adapted by CCNL

Numerosity Follow-up Studies

Follow-up computational work extending the numerosity emergence results, released via Open Science Framework with datasets and code to allow full reproduction and extension by the scientific community.

Testolin, Dolfi, Rochus & Zorzi (2020)

Embodied Numerical Cognition

Research by Mariagrazia Ranzini on embodied cognition and how sensorimotor experience shapes numerical representations, including finger counting, gesture, and the spatial layout of the mental number line.

Ranzini & collaborators

Number Sense in AI Models

Current PhD work by Kuinan Hou examining how number sense develops in artificial neural networks and large language models, bridging computational neuroscience and modern AI research on numerical understanding.

Hou & Zorzi · Ongoing
